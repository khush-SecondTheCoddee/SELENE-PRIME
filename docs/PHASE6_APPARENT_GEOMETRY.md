# Phase 6: station-level apparent body positions

`ApparentPositionService` uses ET seconds past J2000 TDB. It rotates the caller-supplied lunar site vector from a named body-fixed frame into J2000 at reception, adds this to the Moon's barycentric reception position, and iterates the target's barycentric SPICE state at emission until the one-way vacuum light time converges. This computes the station observer geometry directly; it does not subtract a station from a Moon-center apparent direction. Returned records include reception/emission ET, light time, station-relative vector/distance/direction, optional angular radius, iteration count, and correction declaration.

The implemented correction is iterated geometric light time (`LT`) without stellar aberration (`+S`). States are requested with SPICE `NONE` at the needed epochs to avoid applying an aberration correction relative to the SSB and then misusing it for the station. Stellar aberration, relativistic deflection, atmospheric refraction, and an independent comparison with a station-specific SPICE observer are not implemented. The caller must supply a correctly transformed station vector in km and a suitable body-fixed frame supported by the active kernels. No default lunar radii or station datum are embedded.

Synthetic tests independently calculate the retarded position for linearly moving bodies and check station parallax construction, convergence rejection, and angular-radius limits. They test the equations and control flow against analytic fixtures, not genuine kernel coverage or solar-system ephemeris accuracy. Such kernel-backed validation remains pending.
